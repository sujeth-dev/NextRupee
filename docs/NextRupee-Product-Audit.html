1. Product Exploration Summary

A complete inventory of what NextRupee can and cannot do today, based on reading every file in the repository.

What NextRupee Is

NextRupee is a financial decision engine for Indian households. Given a user's money situation (income, expenses, savings, debt, dependents, term insurance, risk tolerance, and goals), it produces the single best next use of their money — ranked deterministically — and proves why with traced evidence and live data.

The product is built around a powerful thesis: "The LLM never computes. Every number traces to arithmetic or a cited source. Same numbers in, same answer out."

A 40-node NetworkX knowledge graph tracking gold price drivers (real yields, DXY, USD/INR, import duty, festival demand, central bank buying, etc.) with 31+ causal edges. Fed by 5 live data sources (Yahoo Finance, FRED API) plus 2 config constants (import duty 6%, domestic premium 1.5%). Backed by 20 authored corpus documents with graph-then-vector RAG retrieval.

Ten Intake Fields

Monthly income, monthly expenses, cash savings, debt amount, debt APR, dependents (binary yes/no), term insurance (binary yes/no), risk tolerance (3 levels), goals (10 options, collected but never used by the engine).

Frontend: Three Pages

Landing page (hero scenario, 3 example cases) → Intake wizard (4 steps) → Results (ranked cards, allocation panel, AskBox with 15 pre-set questions). No authentication, no persistence beyond sessionStorage, no export/print/share.

The Core Problem

NextRupee solves one narrow problem brilliantly — "what's the single best next use of my spare cash?" — but stops short of every question that follows. A user who learns they should prepay debt can't ask "which debt first?" A user told to invest can't ask "which index fund?" A user with 6 months runway can't ask "what about my retirement?" The engine's own transparency principle — "show the work" — creates trust that the product immediately fails to capitalize on because there's nowhere to go next.

2. User Persona Simulations

Five realistic Indian household profiles were simulated through the entire application. Each documents confusions, missing information, dead ends, and opportunities.

Persona 1: Arjun, 22 — First Salary

₹45K/mo income · ₹30K/mo expenses · ₹40K savings · No debt · No dependents · No insurance · Medium risk · Goals: Feel financially safe, Grow long-term wealth

Arjun's Journey

  • Confusion: The intake never asks his age. Age is the single most important variable for retirement planning, insurance pricing, and risk capacity. He's 22 — his risk capacity is high even if his risk tolerance is medium. The engine can't know this.
  • Confusion: "Cash savings you can reach quickly" — does his salary account balance count? What about a liquid fund? The hint says "not locked investments" but doesn't clarify liquid funds.
  • Missing info: He selected "Grow long-term wealth" and "Feel financially safe" as goals, but the results page doesn't reference these goals in any recommendation. The engine ignores them entirely.
  • Dead end: Gets told "Build emergency fund to ₹90,000." Good advice. But then what? No follow-up SIP recommendation, no "come back when you've saved ₹90K," no timeline.
  • Dead end: Clicks "What is an index fund and why are they popular?" in AskBox. Gets a general answer. But can't ask "OK, how do I actually buy one?" or "Which one should I start with?" — guardrails block instrument-level answers.
  • Missing: No education pathway. A 22-year-old needs financial literacy, not just one answer. No learning resources, no glossary, no "basics" section.
  • Missing: No SIP calculator. He earns ₹15K surplus — what does ₹5K/month SIP look like in 10 years? 20 years? The engine computes allocation but never projects growth.
  • Opportunity: First-job users are the highest-LTV users if retained. If NextRupee captured Arjun at 22 and guided him over the next decade, it would own his financial identity.
Persona 2: Priya, 32 — Married, Home Loan, Children

₹1.5L/mo income · ₹1L/mo expenses · ₹5L savings · ₹25L home loan at 8.5% · 2 kids · Has term insurance · Medium risk · Goals: Child's education, House deposit, Grow wealth

Priya's Journey

  • Critical gap: The intake asks "Any high-interest debt?" with a binary yes/no. Priya has a ₹25L home loan at 8.5% APR. Is this "high-interest"? The hint says "credit cards, personal loans." A home loan at 8.5% is economically significant but not "high-interest" in the credit card sense. She answers "No" — and the engine completely ignores a ₹25L liability.
  • Critical gap: Even if she says "Yes" and enters ₹25L at 8.5%, the engine treats it identically to credit card debt. There's no distinction between secured/unsecured, no understanding that home loan prepayment has tax implications (Section 80C, 24b).
  • Missing: "Child's education" goal selected but unused. She can't ask "How much should I save for my kids' college?" or "Is an education plan worth it?" No corpus estimation, no timeline calculation.
  • Missing: No health insurance question. With 2 kids and a single income, health coverage is arguably more urgent than term insurance. The engine never asks about it.
  • Missing: No existing investment tracking. Priya likely has PPF, EPF (via employer), maybe mutual funds. The engine doesn't know about any of this and may recommend actions she's already taken.
  • Missing: "Dependents" is binary. She has 2 kids aged 3 and 7. Number and age of dependents dramatically affect insurance needs, education planning, and risk tolerance timelines.
  • Dead end: Gets allocation recommendation: 55% equity, 30% fixed income, 15% gold. But she's already investing via EPF and PPF (which are "fixed income" and "small savings"). The allocation doesn't account for existing positions.
  • Opportunity: Priya represents the core Indian household user. Solving her complete financial picture (home loan + kids' education + retirement + insurance) would be the killer use case.
Persona 3: Rajesh, 45 — High Income, Multiple Assets

₹4L/mo income · ₹2L/mo expenses · ₹30L savings · No high-interest debt · 2 dependents · Has term insurance · High risk · Goals: Retire comfortably, Grow wealth

Rajesh's Journey

  • Critical gap: Rajesh owns stocks (₹50L), gold (₹10L), FDs (₹20L), PPF (₹15L), EPF (₹25L), and a rental property. The engine knows none of this. It recommends "Invest monthly surplus as 70% equity, 20% fixed income, 10% gold" — but his actual portfolio is already 40% equity, 30% fixed income, 20% gold, 10% real estate. The recommendation is detached from reality.
  • Critical gap: No tax optimization. Rajesh is in the 30% bracket. Section 80C is ₹1.5L cap (already maxed via EPF + PPF). He needs NPS (80CCD), health insurance (80D), HRA optimization, LTCG harvesting. The engine offers nothing here.
  • Missing: No portfolio analysis. He has concentration risk in 3 stocks. No overlap detection, no sector exposure analysis, no rebalancing suggestion.
  • Missing: No retirement readiness score. He's 45, wants to retire at 55. Does he have enough? What's the gap? The engine can't tell him.
  • Missing: No estate planning. He has ₹1.5Cr+ in assets and 2 dependents. No will guidance, no nomination reminder, no joint-account considerations.
  • Dead end: With 15 months runway (₹30L / ₹2L) and no high-interest debt, the engine's top recommendation is "Invest surplus." Correct but unhelpful — he's already investing. He needs optimization, not introduction.
  • Opportunity: High-income users are the premium segment. They need portfolio intelligence, tax optimization, and retirement planning — features worth paying for.
Persona 4: Lakshmi, 62 — Retired

₹0/mo income (pension not captured) · ₹40K/mo expenses · ₹1Cr savings · No debt · No dependents · Has term insurance · Low risk · Goals: Retire comfortably

Lakshmi's Journey

  • Critical failure: The intake asks "monthly income (take-home)" but has no concept of pension, rental income, or investment income. Lakshmi enters ₹0 because she has no salary. This immediately triggers distress mode (surplus ≤ 0), and all investment recommendations are suppressed. She gets "Stabilize cash flow: reduce outgo or raise income." This is wrong — she has ₹1 crore in savings and a ₹30K/month pension.
  • Critical failure: Even if she enters pension as "income," her financial needs are completely different from a salaried person. She needs capital preservation, income generation, and drawdown planning — none of which the engine supports.
  • Missing: No income sources beyond salary (pension, rental, dividends, interest).
  • Missing: No withdrawal rate planning (4% rule, bucket strategy).
  • Missing: No medical expense projection. Health costs rise exponentially after 60.
  • Missing: No inflation impact on fixed income. ₹40K expenses at 6% inflation = ₹72K in 10 years.
  • Missing: "Term insurance" is asked but she's 62 — term insurance at 62 is prohibitively expensive and may not be worth it. The question is irrelevant for her life stage.
  • Opportunity: India's retiree segment is growing rapidly and is vastly underserved by fintech. A retirement-specific module would differentiate NextRupee from every competitor.
Persona 5: Vikram, 35 — NRI in the US

$8K/mo income (₹6.7L) · ₹2L/mo India expenses · ₹15L India savings · No debt · No dependents · No term insurance · High risk · Goals: House deposit, Grow wealth

Vikram's Journey

  • Critical gap: The intake is entirely rupee-denominated. Vikram earns in dollars. He must mentally convert $8K → ₹6.7L at today's exchange rate. If the rupee depreciates 5% next year, his effective income changes — the engine doesn't model this.
  • Missing: No NRI-specific rules. NRIs can't invest in PPF (post-maturity only), have different FD rates (NRE/NRO), need FEMA-compliant investments, face DTAA tax considerations, and have unique repatriation constraints.
  • Missing: No currency allocation. Vikram needs to decide how much to keep in USD vs INR. This is a fundamental allocation decision the engine can't help with.
  • Missing: No understanding of US tax obligations. Vikram may owe US taxes on Indian investments (FBAR, FATCA). The engine's India-only perspective misses this entirely.
  • Missing: "House deposit" goal — is this a house in India or the US? Different markets, different financing, different tax treatment. The engine doesn't ask.
  • Dead end: Allocation suggests 15% gold. But NRIs buying physical gold in India face different import rules. SGBs are available to NRIs through secondary market only. Gold ETFs are the cleanest route — but the engine can't name a specific product.
  • Opportunity: India has 32 million NRIs. NRI financial planning is complex and poorly served by existing tools. A dedicated NRI module would be a major differentiator.

What Would Make Switching Obvious

Critical NextRupee Must Own the "Why" Layer

Every competitor either executes transactions (Zerodha, Groww) or aggregates data (INDmoney). No Indian platform owns the decision layer — the space between "I have money" and "I should buy this specific product." NextRupee's thesis is exactly this gap. But to own it, the engine must answer every financial decision, not just five.

The moat: deterministic, auditable reasoning that no LLM-only product can replicate. Zerodha can't show "why this instead of that" with traced arithmetic. Groww can't prove their recommendations aren't influenced by commission structures. NextRupee can — if it expands its decision surface.

High Build the Trust Graph Before the Transaction Layer

Competitors monetize through transactions. NextRupee's no-commission, no-account model builds trust. The strategy should be: earn trust → capture financial identity → introduce premium intelligence → optionally partner for execution. This is the Morningstar playbook (research → ratings → fund selection → asset management) adapted for Indian retail.

High The Gold Intelligence Engine Is the Prototype for Every Asset Class

The 40-node knowledge graph, graph-then-vector RAG, regime signal, and confidence-capped output — this architecture is a genuine competitive moat. No competitor has anything like it for any asset class. The strategy: replicate this pattern for equity, debt, real estate, and tax — creating a multi-engine intelligence platform where each engine is individually defensible.

Opportunity
Create a comparison engine where users select two instruments and get a personalized analysis factoring in their tax bracket, time horizon, risk tolerance, and existing portfolio.
User benefit
Make confident financial decisions backed by personalized math, not generic advice articles
Business benefit

Technical approach

Market Data Layer Design

Critical R-30: Unified Market Data Abstraction
Problem
Current data layer is hardcoded to 5 Yahoo Finance/FRED endpoints. Adding a new data source requires modifying feeds.py directly. No abstraction, no fallback providers, no normalized schema.
Opportunity
Build a provider-adapter pattern where each data source implements a common interface. Normalized output schema. Automatic fallback when primary provider fails. Background refresh jobs with rate-limit handling.
Technical approach
DataProvider protocol with fetch(driver_id) → DriverValue. Provider registry mapping driver_id → [primary_provider, fallback_provider]. Normalized DriverValue schema: {value, unit, source, fetched_at, as_of, confidence}. Caching layer (Redis or database-backed) with configurable TTL per driver. Background scheduler (APScheduler or Celery-beat) for periodic refresh.
Effort
Hard — 3-4 weeks
Priority
Critical — prerequisite for all engine expansion
Success metrics
New data source can be added in < 1 day, zero downtime during provider outages

Final Assessment

NextRupee has a genuinely defensible thesis: deterministic, evidence-traced financial recommendations where the LLM never computes. This is architecturally sound and competitively unique. No Indian fintech platform offers anything like the "show the work" transparency model backed by a knowledge graph and dual guardrails.

The product's limitation is not quality — it's scope. It solves one problem brilliantly (the "next best action" for spare cash) but stops at the exact moment users want to go deeper. The gold intelligence engine is a working prototype for how every asset class should be modeled. The rules engine hierarchy is elegant and well-tested. The eval harness is more rigorous than most production fintech products.

The strategic opportunity is clear: replicate the gold engine pattern across equity, debt, tax, insurance, and real estate. Add user accounts for retention. Build the comparison engine for engagement. Add goal tracking for habit formation. Layer on the research platform for daily usage.

The path from "impressive portfolio project" to "India's most intelligent wealth platform" is not about fixing bugs or adding polish — it's about expanding the decision surface while preserving the core principles that make NextRupee trustworthy.
