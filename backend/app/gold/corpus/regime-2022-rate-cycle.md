title: "Regime study: the 2022–2023 hiking cycle and the central-bank bid"
tags: [regime, rates, macro, flows, structural]
---

2022 should have been terrible for gold. The Federal Reserve delivered the fastest
hiking cycle in four decades; US 10-year real yields swung from about −1% to +1.5%,
and the dollar index touched a 20-year high near 114. By the historical real-yield
playbook, gold should have fallen 25–30%. It fell about 20% from its March peak to
its November trough — and then finished the year roughly flat, near $1,800/oz.

The gap between the model and the outcome was official-sector buying. Central banks —
led by emerging-market reserve managers after the freezing of Russian FX reserves in
February 2022 — bought a record ~1,080 tonnes in 2022 and continued near that pace in
2023–2024. This buying is price-insensitive and structural: it responds to sanctions
risk and reserve diversification, not to real yields. It effectively put a floor under
gold and weakened (without breaking) the real-yield correlation that had held since
2006.

The episode forced a model update, reflected in the driver graph: real yields and the
dollar remain the strongest cyclical drivers, but geopolitical risk now transmits
through a persistent central-bank-buying node, not just through episodic safe-haven
flows. Any confident "gold must fall because real yields rose" claim now carries an
explicit invalidator: sustained official-sector accumulation.

For Indian savers the 2022 lesson was familiar in one more way: the rupee depreciated
from ~₹74 to ~₹83/USD, so INR gold made new highs in a year USD gold went nowhere.
