Indian savers can hold gold exposure through several routes with very different cost
structures; the differences are frequently larger than a year of gold returns.

Physical jewellery carries making charges (typically 8–25% of value), 3% GST, the
import-duty wedge, and a buyback haircut. As an investment vehicle it is dominated by
every alternative; its purchase is better modelled as consumption with salvage value.

Physical coins/bars avoid making charges but keep GST, duty exposure, storage risk,
and dealer spreads.

Gold ETFs and gold mutual funds track the landed price with expense ratios around
0.5–1% a year, held in demat/fund form.

Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs), issued by the RBI 2015–2024, were the strongest
risk-adjusted route while primary tranches existed: price-linked to 999 gold, plus
2.5% annual interest, capital-gains tax exempt at maturity, sovereign credit.
The programme's primary issuance was discontinued after FY2024-25 (the government
judged it an expensive borrowing route), so availability is now secondary-market
only, often at premiums to fair value and with thin liquidity.

Decision relevance: when an allocation calls for gold, the vehicle question is
second-order for the ranking but first-order for execution cost. NextRupee
deliberately recommends the asset class and surfaces the cost structure of routes as
evidence, without naming specific funds or products — vehicle-level selection is
where advice regulation begins and where this product stops.
