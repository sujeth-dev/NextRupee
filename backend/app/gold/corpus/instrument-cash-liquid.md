title: "Cash, savings accounts, and liquid funds: the money you can reach today"

---

Cash-like holdings are the base of every plan: a savings account, a sweep-in deposit,
or a liquid/overnight mutual fund. Their job is not to grow money but to be available
instantly and without loss, so an emergency or a near-term bill never forces you to
sell a long-term investment at a bad time.

A savings account in India typically pays around 3–4% a year and is fully liquid.
Liquid and overnight funds invest in very short-term, high-quality debt and have
historically returned a little more — often in the 5–7% range depending on the rate
cycle — while still allowing redemption in a day (many offer instant redemption up to a
small limit). They carry mild risk: unlike a bank deposit they are not guaranteed, but
the class is among the least volatile in investing.

The right amount to keep here is your emergency fund — commonly three to six months of
expenses — plus any money you will spend within a year. Holding much more than that is
a quiet cost: cash typically loses to inflation, so an over-large cash pile shrinks in
real terms every year it sits idle.

Decision relevance: cash is sized to a need, not to a return target. Once the emergency
buffer and near-term bills are covered, additional money is better matched to its time
horizon in a growth or income class. NextRupee treats the cash layer as the safety base
that must be secure before it recommends any investing.
