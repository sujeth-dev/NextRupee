title: "Fixed and recurring deposits: a locked, guaranteed return"
tags: [instrument, fd, income]
---

A fixed deposit (FD) hands a bank a lump sum for a chosen term — a few months to a few
years — in exchange for a fixed interest rate known upfront. A recurring deposit (RD)
does the same with a fixed monthly contribution instead of a lump sum. Both are among
the safest places to hold money in India: deposits up to ₹5 lakh per bank are insured
by the RBI's deposit insurance (DICGC).

FD rates move with the RBI's rate cycle and typically sit around 6–7.5% for retail
savers, with senior citizens usually earning about 0.5% more. The certainty is the
appeal — you know the exact maturity value the day you invest — but it comes with two
real costs. First, the interest is fully taxable at your income-tax slab, so a saver in
a higher slab may keep only a little above inflation. Second, the money is locked;
breaking an FD early usually means a rate penalty.

FDs suit money you will need on a known date within a few years — a planned purchase, a
child's fee due next year, or the stable slice of a larger plan — and they suit savers
who value certainty over higher expected returns. They are a poor vehicle for very
long-horizon goals, where the fixed, tax-inefficient return tends to lag growth assets.

Decision relevance: an FD is a certainty tool, not a growth tool. NextRupee may point
toward the deposit family for near-term, must-not-fall money, while keeping longer money
matched to its horizon — and it never recommends a particular bank or scheme.
