India runs a family of government-backed savings schemes aimed at long-horizon, safety-
first goals. The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a 15-year account with a government-set
rate (revised quarterly, historically around 7–8%), where the interest and maturity are
tax-free and contributions qualify for a tax deduction — one of the few genuinely
tax-free compounding vehicles available to households. The Employees' Provident Fund
(EPF) is the payroll-linked retirement pool for salaried workers, with employer matching
and a similar government-declared rate.

Other schemes serve specific needs: the Sukanya Samriddhi account for a girl child's
future, the Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme for retirees seeking dependable income, and
the National Pension System (NPS) for a low-cost, market-linked retirement corpus with
its own tax benefits.

The shared trait is safety plus a lock-in. Capital is effectively government-guaranteed
and the returns beat a taxable FD for many savers because of the tax treatment — but the
money is committed for years, so these are unsuitable for anything you might need soon.
Their rates are administered, not market-set, so they will not capture equity-like
growth over decades.

Decision relevance: small-savings schemes are the safe, tax-efficient backbone of a
long-term plan, especially for conservative savers and retirement. They complement, not
replace, growth assets over long horizons. NextRupee reflects the class generically and
does not advise on individual scheme selection or tax filing.
