title: "Commodities beyond gold: silver, energy, and why they rarely fit a household"
tags: [instrument, commodities, growth]
---

Commodities are raw physical goods — silver, crude oil, industrial metals, agricultural
produce — traded on exchanges, usually through futures contracts or specialised funds
rather than by holding the physical good. Their prices swing on global supply and demand,
weather, and geopolitics, and they pay no interest or dividend along the way.

Silver is the commodity households encounter most. It behaves partly like gold — a
store of value in uncertain times — but is far more volatile because roughly half its
demand is industrial, tying it to the economic cycle. Energy and agricultural
commodities are more volatile still and are dominated by professional traders; for a
household they are closer to speculation than investment.

Because commodities produce no income and their long-run real return has historically
been near zero, they are not a wealth-building class. Their only defensible household
role is a small diversifier: some commodities, like gold, can hold up when equities and
currencies weaken. Gold already fills that hedge role more reliably and with lower
volatility, which is why a household rarely needs a separate commodities allocation.

Decision relevance: outside gold, commodities are a specialist, high-volatility class
with no income and weak long-run returns — generally not part of a household plan.
NextRupee surfaces them for context, keeps any hedge role centred on gold, and never
recommends a specific commodity, contract, or fund.
