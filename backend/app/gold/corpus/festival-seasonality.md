title: "Reference: festival and wedding seasonality in Indian gold demand"
tags: [india, seasonal, price]
---

Indian gold demand is strongly seasonal, and the seasonality is demand-side, not
price-side — a distinction that matters for household decisions.

The demand calendar: Akshaya Tritiya (April–May) and the Dhanteras/Diwali window
(October–November) are the two auspicious buying peaks, followed by the
November–February wedding season. The September–December quarter regularly accounts
for a third or more of annual jewellery demand. Rural cash flows amplify this: a good
monsoon harvest (kharif income arrives September–November) historically adds several
percentage points to festival-quarter demand.

What seasonality does NOT reliably do is move the headline price. India is a price
taker in a global market; festival demand shows up primarily in the DOMESTIC PREMIUM
over landed cost, which typically firms by $2–10/oz into Dhanteras, and in making
charges and dealer spreads, which rise with showroom traffic. The international price
shows no dependable Diwali pattern.

Decision relevance: "should I buy gold for Diwali?" decomposes into (a) an allocation
question — does gold belong in the portfolio at its current share? — which has nothing
to do with the calendar, and (b) an execution question — buying during the festival
window means paying a seasonal premium and peak making charges. If the allocation case
holds, buying slightly before or after the peak window, or via a non-physical route,
avoids the seasonal wedge. If the allocation case does not hold, the festival is
consumption, and should be budgeted as such rather than justified as investment.
