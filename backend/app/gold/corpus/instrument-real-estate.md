title: "Real estate: large, illiquid, and part-consumption"
tags: [instrument, realestate, growth]
---

Property is the asset most Indian households aspire to, but as an investment it behaves
very differently from a fund or deposit. A home is a single, indivisible, expensive
asset that can take months to sell, carries high transaction costs (stamp duty,
registration, brokerage — often 7–10% round-trip), and demands ongoing maintenance,
tax, and sometimes an EMI. Its price is local and opaque: two flats in the same city can
move in opposite directions.

The home you live in is best treated as part investment, part consumption — it houses
you, so its "return" is partly the rent you avoid paying, not pure profit. A second
property bought purely to let out is a genuine investment, but its rental yield in
Indian cities is often modest (commonly 2–4% a year) and the bulk of any gain rides on
price appreciation, which has been uneven across cycles.

Households wanting property exposure without the lumpiness and illiquidity can use REITs
(Real Estate Investment Trusts) — exchange-listed vehicles that own income-producing
commercial property and pay out most of their rent, tradable like a share in small
amounts. They give a slice of real estate with daily liquidity and diversification.

Decision relevance: direct real estate is illiquid, concentrated, and cost-heavy, so it
suits only those who can commit a large sum for many years; REITs offer a lighter
alternative. NextRupee treats real estate as one class among several and never
recommends a specific property, builder, or REIT.
