title: "Bonds: lending money for a fixed income"

A bond is a loan you make to a borrower — a government or a company — that pays you
regular interest (the coupon) and returns the principal on a set date. In India the
safest are government securities (G-Secs) and RBI-backed instruments like the Floating
Rate Savings Bond; corporate bonds pay more but carry the issuer's credit risk, and
that extra yield is compensation for the chance the borrower cannot repay.

Most households access bonds not one at a time but through debt mutual funds, which hold
a diversified basket. Two risks matter. Credit risk is the borrower defaulting — kept
low by sticking to government and high-rated issuers. Interest-rate risk is subtler: when
market rates rise, existing bonds paying old lower rates fall in price, so even a "safe"
bond fund can dip in the short run. Longer-maturity bonds swing more; short-duration
funds are steadier.

Expected returns typically fall between cash and equity — often broadly in line with FDs
over time, but with daily liquidity and, for some categories, more favourable tax
treatment on longer holdings. Bonds earn their place by cushioning a portfolio: they
tend to hold up, or even rise, when equities fall, smoothing the overall ride.

Decision relevance: bonds are the income-and-stability slice — steadier than equity,
more flexible than a locked FD, riskier than cash. NextRupee treats them at the asset-
class level and does not recommend a specific bond, issuer, or debt fund.
