title: "Reference: the structure of global and Indian gold demand"
tags: [structural, flows, india]
---

Global gold demand runs at roughly 4,400–4,900 tonnes a year (World Gold Council
data). The composition explains most of gold's behaviour: jewellery ~40–45%
(price-elastic, concentrated in India and China), investment ~25% (bars, coins, ETFs —
price-momentum-following), central banks ~20–25% since 2022 (price-insensitive,
structurally rising), and technology ~7% (stable). Supply is ~75% mine production —
famously inelastic, since a new mine takes a decade — and ~25% recycling, which is the
true short-run supply valve: old-gold selling surges when prices spike, as Indian
households demonstrated in 2020.

India-specific structure: annual Indian consumption of 700–850 tonnes is almost
entirely imported, which is why gold sits at the centre of India's trade-deficit
politics and attracts duty interventions. Indian household holdings are estimated
around 24,000–25,000 tonnes — the world's largest private stock — meaning recycling
capacity is enormous and domestic demand is as much about liquidity preference as
accumulation.

Two decision-relevant asymmetries emerge. First, marginal demand (investment + central
banks) sets the price at the margin; jewellery adjusts to price rather than driving
it. So headlines about wedding-season demand explain premia, not price direction.
Second, because supply cannot respond, sustained shifts in investment or official
demand move prices a long way — the market clears on price, not quantity. This is why
regime identification (which demand segment is in charge) matters more than any single
statistic, and why the driver graph routes flows and central-bank nodes into the price
with heavier weights than physical-demand nodes.
