title: "Structural note: rupee depreciation and the INR gold return"
tags: [india, currency, structural, allocation]
---

A quarter to a third of the long-run INR gold return has come not from gold but from
the rupee. USD/INR moved from ~₹45 in 2010 to ~₹63 in 2015, ~₹74 in 2020, and through
₹83–87 in 2024–25 — roughly 3–4% annualised depreciation over long windows, in line
with the India–US inflation differential (purchasing-power parity holds loosely but
persistently for INR).

This has three consequences for a household. First, INR gold is meaningfully less
volatile in drawdowns than USD gold, because global stress episodes that hit the USD
price usually also weaken the rupee (2008, 2013, 2020, 2022), and the fx move cushions
the local price. Second, long-run INR gold returns exceed USD gold returns by roughly
the depreciation rate — gold has functioned partly as an fx hedge for Indian savers,
which is a legitimate, structural component of its allocation case. Third, the hedge
is against rupee weakness specifically: in the rare windows of rupee strength, INR
gold underperforms.

The depreciation trend is a slow structural driver, not a timing signal: annual moves
are dominated by RBI intervention, oil prices, and capital flows that resist
short-term prediction. In the driver graph, INR depreciation therefore carries a
strong positive edge into INR gold but no edge into any timing node.

Claim discipline: any statement like "gold protects against rupee depreciation" must
cite this structural note, and any quantified fx forecast is out of scope — the
engine reports the current level and trend direction only.
