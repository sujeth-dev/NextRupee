title: "Shares (equity): owning a slice of businesses"
tags: [instrument, equity, growth]
---

Buying a share makes you a part-owner of a company, entitled to a share of its future
profits through price growth and dividends. Equity is the growth engine of a household
plan: over long stretches it has historically outpaced every other liquid class,
because a diversified basket of businesses grows earnings faster than inflation.

That return is payment for enduring risk. Equity is volatile — broad markets can fall
30–50% in a bad year and take time to recover — and individual companies can fail
outright. The single biggest mistake households make is selling into a fall and locking
in the loss; the second is holding too few stocks, so one company's collapse sinks the
plan. Both are why most people are better served owning the whole market cheaply (see
index funds) than picking individual shares.

Equity only makes sense for money you will not need for at least seven years, because
that is roughly the horizon over which its higher expected return has historically
overwhelmed its short-term swings. Money needed sooner belongs in steadier classes. In
India, long-term equity gains carry a relatively light tax, which modestly favours the
patient holder.

Decision relevance: equity is for long-horizon growth money only, and diversification
plus staying invested matter more than selection. NextRupee recommends the equity asset
class and its share of a plan — never a specific stock, sector, or fund — because
picking individual securities is exactly where investment advice is regulated.
