title: "Regime study: India's 2013 import duty hikes and the premium/smuggling cycle"
tags: [regime, india, policy, duty, price]
---

2013 is the reference case for how Indian policy, not global markets, can dominate the
local gold price. Facing a record current-account deficit — gold was India's
second-largest import after oil — the government raised the import duty in steps from
2% (January 2012) to 10% (August 2013), and the RBI's 80:20 rule tied imports to
re-export obligations.

Three effects followed, all visible in the driver graph. First, the duty acted as a
direct price wedge: landed cost rose one-for-one with the duty, so Indian buyers paid
about 10% over the international price before local premia. Second, official imports
collapsed while smuggling surged — seizure data and premium behaviour implied a large
grey market, because a 10% duty is a 10% margin for an unofficial channel. Third, the
domestic premium over landed price became volatile: it spiked above $100/oz during
supply squeezes in late 2013, then compressed when grey supply or duty cuts restored
availability.

The unwind was equally instructive: duty came down in later budgets (notably to 6% in
July 2024), and each cut showed up immediately in local prices and compressed premia.

For a household decision today the lesson is: the duty level is a known, policy-set
component of the INR price. When duty is high, part of what you pay for physical gold
is a tax wedge that a future budget can remove — a one-way downside risk to resale
value that has nothing to do with global gold. This is why duty changes appear as an
explicit invalidation condition on gold-related recommendations.
