title: "Reference: Indian gold import duty timeline and price mechanics"
tags: [india, policy, duty, price]
---

The import duty is a policy-set component of every rupee gold price. Mechanics: the
landed INR price ≈ USD spot × USD/INR × (1 + duty) ÷ 31.1035 × 10 for a 10-gram
quote, plus a market-determined domestic premium or discount, plus GST (3% on value)
at retail.

Timeline of the duty on refined gold bars: ~1% until January 2012; raised in steps —
2% (Jan 2012), 4% (Mar 2012), 6% (Jan 2013), 8% (Jun 2013), 10% (Aug 2013) — as a
current-account-deficit measure. Held around 10% for years (with cess restructurings:
12.5% basic in July 2019, recomposed to an effective ~15% including cess in July
2022's package, where the basic rate moved to 12.5% plus AIDC). Then the July 2024
budget cut the effective rate sharply to 6% (basic + cess) — the largest single cut on
record — explicitly to curb smuggling and revive official channels; domestic prices
dropped ~6% within days.

Two standing implications. First, duty risk is asymmetric for a buyer: a future cut
lowers the resale value of already-purchased gold by roughly the cut size, while hikes
are historically associated with deficit stress you cannot predict. Second, the wider
the duty, the larger the grey-market incentive, and the less reliable the official
premium as a demand signal.

Any recommendation that involves buying physical gold should therefore carry "import
duty changes in a budget announcement" as an invalidation condition, and quote the
duty component of the current price explicitly.
