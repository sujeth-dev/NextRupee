title: "Regime study: gold through the 2008 global financial crisis"

Gold's behaviour in 2008 is the canonical case study in why it is a crisis hedge with a
caveat. In the acute phase of the crisis (September–November 2008), gold initially FELL
roughly 20% from its March peak alongside every other asset. The reason was mechanical,
not fundamental: leveraged investors facing margin calls sold whatever was liquid, and
gold is among the most liquid assets on any book. Safe-haven status does not exempt an
asset from a liquidity crunch.

The recovery told the second half of the story. As central banks cut rates toward zero
and launched quantitative easing, real yields collapsed. Gold bottomed in November 2008
near $710/oz and more than doubled over the following three years, peaking above
$1,900/oz in September 2011. Holding-cost logic drove this: when the real return on
safe bonds is negative, the zero yield on gold stops being a disadvantage.

For Indian households the 2008 episode had a second layer: the rupee depreciated from
about ₹39/USD in early 2008 to about ₹52/USD by March 2009, so INR gold fell far less
than USD gold during the crash and rose far more in the recovery. This asymmetry — INR
depreciation cushioning USD gold drawdowns — repeats in nearly every global stress
episode and is a structural reason gold behaves differently for an Indian saver than
for a dollar-based investor.

Lessons encoded in the driver graph: risk sentiment moves gold in both directions
(liquidation first, hedge bid later); real yields dominate the medium term; USD/INR
translates global moves into local outcomes with a persistent depreciation bias.
