title: "Gold as an asset class: a hedge, not a growth engine"
tags: [instrument, gold]
---

Within a household plan, gold plays one specific role: it is a hedge that tends to hold
its value — or rise — when currencies weaken, inflation runs hot, or markets and
geopolitics turn frightening. It is not a growth engine. Over very long periods gold has
roughly tracked inflation rather than building real wealth the way equity does, and it
pays no interest or dividend while you hold it.

Its value in a plan comes from behaving differently from equity. Because gold often
zigs when shares zag, a modest slice can steady the overall portfolio and give a saver
something that holds up in exactly the years stocks fall — which also makes it
psychologically easier to stay invested. For Indian households there is an added
currency effect: gold is priced globally in dollars, so a falling rupee lifts the local
price even when world gold is flat.

Because it is a diversifier rather than a driver, gold is usually sized as a small share
of a plan — commonly a single-digit to low-teens percentage — not a core holding.
Buying it in a rush at festival-season premiums, or as high-making-charge jewellery,
quietly erodes that role.

Decision relevance: gold earns a small, steady place as insurance inside a diversified
plan, not a bet to time. NextRupee recommends the asset class and its share, surfaces
