title: "Structural note: why real yields are gold's gravity"
tags: [macro, rates, structural, allocation]
---

The single most reliable medium-term relationship in gold pricing is the inverse link
to US real yields — the yield on Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities. From 2006
through 2021 the correlation between the 10-year TIPS yield and the USD gold price was
strongly negative; every major bull leg (2008–2011, 2019–2020) coincided with real
yields falling, and the 2013 "taper tantrum" crash coincided with a violent real-yield
rise.

The mechanism is opportunity cost. Gold pays no coupon. Its competitor as a
store-of-value is the safest real asset available: an inflation-protected government
bond. When that bond pays +2% real, holding gold costs you 2% a year in forgone real
return. When it pays −1% real, gold's zero real carry is a premium product. The market
prices this continuously, which is why the LEVEL of real yields matters more than the
level of inflation — gold is an inflation hedge only through the real-rate channel.

Two structural caveats. First, the 2022–2024 period weakened the correlation:
price-insensitive central-bank buying absorbed the real-yield headwind (see the 2022
regime study). The relationship now works with a floor under it. Second, for an Indian
holder the transmission is two-step: US real yields set the USD price, and USD/INR
translates it; rupee depreciation has historically offset much of the real-yield drag
in INR terms.

In the driver graph, real yields carry the strongest negative edge into USD gold, fed
by nominal yields (+) and inflation expectations (−), with Fed policy upstream of both.
